Why seven joints

A six-joint arm has exactly enough freedom to place its tool at any position and orientation in its workspace. A seventh joint adds redundancy: the arm can reach the same pose in many different ways, which lets it move its elbow around obstacles, stay away from joint limits and singular configurations, and work more like a human arm.

That extra freedom is also what makes the project hard. The inverse kinematics no longer has a single answer, so the planner and controller have to choose between infinitely many valid arm configurations.

How I built it

Mechanical design

Designed the links, joints and mounts of the arm in CAD, then manufactured and assembled the parts into the physical manipulator.

Tools: SolidWorks

Kinematics

Modelled the forward and inverse kinematics of the seven-joint chain, handling the redundancy that a seventh joint introduces.

Tools: MATLAB

Motion planning & simulation

Planned and validated trajectories in simulation before a single motor moved on the real hardware.

Tools: ROS · MoveIt · RViz · Python

Control

Implemented the control of the joints on the real arm, closing the loop between the planned trajectory and the hardware.

Tools: MATLAB · Python
